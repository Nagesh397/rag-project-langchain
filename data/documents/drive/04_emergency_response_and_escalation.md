## 1. Purpose and Recognition

This policy provides a common framework for recognizing deterioration, activating help, responding to emergencies, and transferring responsibility safely. It applies to all hospital locations, including clinical units, clinics, diagnostic areas, waiting rooms, parking areas, and administrative spaces. Staff are expected to act within training and scope while calling for qualified help early.

Warning signs may include a new change in consciousness, severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, seizure, uncontrolled bleeding, signs of stroke, severe allergic reaction, sudden deterioration in vital signs, or a credible report of imminent harm. Any staff member may call for emergency assistance when a person appears unstable. A call should not be delayed while seeking permission.

The first responder assesses immediate danger, calls the designated emergency number, identifies the location, begins appropriate basic support, and sends someone for emergency equipment. Staff maintain privacy and keep bystanders away when possible.

## 2. Medical Emergency Response

When a medical emergency is activated, the responding team brings required equipment, assigns a team leader, and confirms the patient identity and location. Staff provide concise information using the hospital handoff format: current concern, relevant background, assessment, actions taken, and response. One person records times, medications, interventions, and key decisions.

The team leader establishes priorities and delegates tasks clearly. Only qualified staff perform advanced interventions. Equipment is checked after use, disposable items are replaced, and medications are reconciled. If the patient is transferred, the receiving location receives a complete handoff and the sending team documents the disposition.

Family members receive appropriate support and communication from the designated clinician or liaison. Staff should not share unverified information or discuss another patient in public areas. After the emergency, the team completes required clinical documentation and safety reporting.

## 3. Fire, Evacuation, and Non-Clinical Emergencies

For fire or smoke, staff activate the alarm, call the emergency number, remove people from immediate danger when safe, close doors, and follow the hospital’s evacuation sequence. Staff do not use elevators when prohibited and do not re-enter until authorized. Patient movement priorities are determined by the incident commander and local emergency plan.

For hazardous spills, violence, suspicious packages, utility failure, or other non-clinical emergencies, staff protect themselves and others, call the designated response number, isolate the area when safe, and follow instructions from security, facilities, or the incident command team. Do not touch suspicious items or attempt cleanup without authorization and appropriate protection.

The hospital maintains business-continuity procedures for power, water, oxygen, information systems, communications, and supply disruption. Department leaders keep current contact lists and downtime procedures accessible when normal systems are unavailable.

## 4. Rapid Response and Escalation of Deterioration

Clinical staff monitor patients according to the care plan and escalate concerns when observations, trends, or clinical judgment indicate deterioration. A rapid response call may be made by staff, patients, or families according to local process. The caller provides the exact location, patient identifier, concern, and callback information.

The responsible nurse or clinician remains with the patient until the response team arrives and gives a structured handoff. The team assesses, treats within scope, and decides whether the patient remains in the current location, transfers to a higher-acuity area, or requires another emergency pathway. Unresolved concerns must be escalated through the chain of command.

A patient or family request for urgent review must be taken seriously. Staff should acknowledge the concern, assess the patient, obtain help, and document the response. A disagreement about escalation must never result in delayed care.

## 5. Drills, Recovery, and Review

The emergency preparedness committee maintains drills and exercises appropriate to the hospital’s hazards. Drills test communication, equipment, staffing, accessibility, evacuation, documentation, and coordination with external responders. Findings are recorded with assigned owners and due dates.

After a significant emergency, leaders support clinical recovery, staff debriefing, patient and family communication, equipment replacement, and environmental restoration. A post-event review identifies what worked, what failed, and what should change. The review is conducted in a learning-focused manner while preserving required accountability.

Emergency contacts, assembly points, equipment locations, and response procedures are controlled documents. Staff must use the current approved version. This policy does not replace local emergency numbers, fire code requirements, or clinical protocols for specific conditions.
