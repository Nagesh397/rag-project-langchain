## 1. Principles and Standard Precautions

Infection prevention is the responsibility of every person working in or visiting the hospital. Standard precautions apply to all patients regardless of known infection status. They include hand hygiene, risk-based personal protective equipment, respiratory hygiene, safe injection practice, sharps safety, environmental cleaning, and appropriate handling of linen and waste.

Staff must perform hand hygiene before touching a patient, before a clean or aseptic task, after body-fluid exposure risk, after touching a patient, and after touching the patient environment. Alcohol-based hand rub is preferred when hands are not visibly soiled; soap and water are required in situations identified by infection prevention guidance and when hands are visibly dirty.

Artificial nails, jewelry, skin conditions, and uniforms must be managed according to department requirements. Hand hygiene stations must remain accessible and stocked. Leaders are responsible for observing compliance and addressing barriers without creating a punitive reporting environment.

## 2. Transmission-Based Precautions

Additional precautions are used when the route of transmission creates risk beyond standard precautions. The treating team contacts infection prevention when a patient may require contact, droplet, airborne, or special isolation precautions. Precautions begin promptly based on clinical information and are adjusted when test results and expert review are available.

Patients requiring precautions should receive clear instructions and appropriate signage. Staff select protective equipment before entering the care area, use it correctly, and remove it in a way that prevents contamination. Dedicated or appropriately disinfected equipment should be used whenever possible. Transport is limited to medically necessary movement, and the receiving department is informed of required precautions.

Visitors receive instructions appropriate to the precaution type. The hospital balances infection control with compassionate visitation and patient rights. Isolation decisions should not be used as a substitute for a clinical assessment or environmental cleaning.

## 3. Aseptic Practice and Device Safety

Aseptic technique is required for procedures where contamination could cause infection. Staff prepare equipment and the patient using approved methods, maintain a clean field, and avoid touching key parts. If sterility is uncertain, the item is treated as contaminated and replaced or reprocessed through the approved route.

Intravascular lines, urinary catheters, respiratory equipment, surgical sites, and other devices are assessed daily for continued need. Unnecessary devices are removed promptly. Dressing changes, access procedures, specimen collection, and device maintenance follow department-specific procedures and manufacturer instructions.

Reusable equipment must be cleaned and disinfected between patients using an approved product and contact time. Single-use items are not reused. Staff report damaged packaging, failed sterilization indicators, equipment contamination, or supply shortages immediately to the supervisor and infection prevention team.

## 4. Exposure, Outbreak, and Occupational Health Response

A suspected occupational exposure requires immediate first aid and prompt notification to the supervisor and occupational health service. The exposed person should not delay reporting while waiting for symptoms. Confidential evaluation, testing, prophylaxis, and follow-up are arranged according to the exposure type and applicable requirements.

Clusters of unusual illness, unexpected pathogens, increased surgical-site infections, or suspected outbreaks must be reported immediately to infection prevention. Staff preserve relevant specimens and records, follow case-finding instructions, and avoid informal announcements that may cause misinformation. Infection prevention coordinates communication with clinical leaders and public health authorities when required.

Staff with symptoms that may create transmission risk must follow occupational health instructions. Return-to-work decisions are based on current guidance, role, symptoms, and risk. Patient confidentiality is protected during all communications.

## 5. Surveillance, Training, and Accountability

The infection prevention team monitors healthcare-associated infections, hand hygiene, device utilization, environmental practices, antimicrobial resistance, and compliance with precautions. Findings are reported to clinical and executive committees with trends, risk interpretation, and corrective actions.

Every worker completes infection-prevention training during onboarding and at the required recurring interval. High-risk departments receive additional training and competency validation. Supervisors provide coaching at the point of care and escalate repeated unsafe practice through the normal performance process.

This policy is reviewed at least annually and after a significant outbreak, regulatory update, or change in clinical practice. The current version is controlled by the Infection Prevention and Control Committee. Questions about isolation, cleaning, exposure, or outbreak response should be directed to infection prevention rather than resolved through guesswork.
