# Privacy, Confidentiality, and Information Security Policy

**Document ID:** PIS-005  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Privacy and Information Security Office  
**Classification:** Internal hospital policy  
**Effective date:** 2026-01-01

## 1. Purpose and Minimum Necessary Access

This policy protects patient, staff, business, and operational information from inappropriate access, use, disclosure, alteration, or loss. It applies to employees, contractors, students, volunteers, vendors, and any person using hospital information systems or handling hospital records. Access is granted for a defined work purpose and must be limited to the minimum necessary information.

Users receive individual credentials and must not share passwords, tokens, badges, or multi-factor authentication approvals. Access must be locked or logged out when a workstation is unattended. Users may access a record because their job requires it, not because they are curious, know the person, or are involved in a personal matter.

The hospital maintains role-based access and reviews access when a person changes role, leaves the organization, or no longer requires a system. Managers are responsible for confirming that access remains appropriate.

## 2. Appropriate Use and Disclosure

Information may be used for treatment, payment, operations, approved research, legal obligations, or another authorized purpose. Staff verify identity and authority before discussing or releasing information. Conversations about patients must occur in private enough settings to reduce unintended disclosure. Screens, printed reports, labels, and whiteboards must be positioned and handled appropriately.

Requests from patients, families, insurers, law enforcement, journalists, employers, or other organizations must follow the approved release process. Staff should refer uncertain requests to the privacy office rather than making an informal decision. A family relationship or verbal claim does not automatically establish authorization.

Only approved systems and communication channels may be used for hospital information. Personal email, consumer messaging applications, unapproved cloud storage, and removable media must not be used for confidential information. Images and recordings require approved purpose, consent where required, secure storage, and controlled sharing.

## 3. Security Controls and RAG Use

Hospital systems use strong authentication, encryption where available, endpoint protection, backup, logging, and controlled network access. Users must install updates through approved processes and report suspicious messages, unexpected prompts, lost devices, and suspected malware promptly. Never approve a login request that was not initiated by you.

Information entered into the hospital knowledge assistant must be approved for that tool and must follow role-based access. Users must not paste identifiable patient data into an unapproved prompt or attempt to retrieve records outside their job function. The assistant is a support tool; it does not replace the clinical record, professional judgment, or emergency response.

The RAG system must preserve document permissions during retrieval, show source references, record appropriate audit metadata, and prevent one user from seeing restricted content through a generated answer. Secrets such as API keys and database credentials belong in protected environment configuration, never in source code or documents.

## 4. Incident Reporting and Response

A suspected privacy or security incident includes misdirected email, lost device, unauthorized access, credential compromise, malware, accidental disclosure, inappropriate browsing, or suspicious data extraction. Report it immediately to the service desk, privacy office, or information security team using the approved channel. Do not delete evidence or investigate beyond your role.

If credentials may be compromised, disconnect or secure the affected device as instructed and change credentials through the approved process. If information was sent to the wrong recipient, notify the response team and follow instructions; do not send additional confidential information while attempting to correct the mistake.

The response team assesses scope, containment, evidence, affected systems, required notifications, and corrective action. Staff must cooperate with the review and protect confidentiality during the investigation. Retaliation for good-faith reporting is prohibited.

## 5. Training, Retention, and Accountability

All users complete privacy and security training before access and at the required recurring interval. Additional training is required for privileged administrators, clinical leaders, developers, analysts, and users of sensitive systems. The hospital may use simulated exercises and access audits to reinforce safe behavior.

Records are retained and destroyed according to the approved schedule, legal holds, clinical requirements, and business needs. Disposal must make information unreadable or unrecoverable. Backups and exported datasets are subject to the same protection requirements as source records.

Violations may result in coaching, access restriction, disciplinary action, contract action, regulatory reporting, or legal response depending on severity and applicable rules. This policy is reviewed after major system changes, significant incidents, and changes in law or organizational risk.
